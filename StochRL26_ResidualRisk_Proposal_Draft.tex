\section{Motivation and problem definition}
A risk controller can reduce drawdown by suppressing profitable trades. Small allocation corrections need not have small financial consequences, and an average-return constraint allows successful interventions to offset harmful ones. This project asks: \emph{Can a personalized residual controller reduce portfolio drawdown while explicitly budgeting the positive net-return shortfall caused by its interventions?}

Consider a small price-taking investor allocating among three Korean ETFs and cash, without leverage or short selling. Market history $h_t$ is exogenous, but holdings, wealth, costs, and drawdown depend on actions. Decisions use close-of-day information and execute at the next open. For a fixed $T$-day episode $\omega$, simulate an untouched reference policy $\pi_0$ and a corrected policy $\pi_\phi$ on the same market path, from identical initial capital and positions. Each account maintains its own subsequent holdings and costs. Define
\begin{equation}
 G_i(\omega)=\log\frac{V^i_T}{V^i_0},\qquad
 L_\phi(\omega)=\bigl[G_0(\omega)-G_\phi(\omega)\bigr]_+,\quad [u]_+=\max(0,u).
\end{equation}
Here $G_i$ includes trading costs. The proposed adaptation minimizes expected maximum drawdown $D_T$ subject to an intervention-loss budget:
\begin{equation}
 \min_\phi\;\mathbb E[D_T(\pi_\phi)]
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad \mathbb E[L_\phi]\leq\epsilon.
\end{equation}
The budget is measured in cumulative log-return units over the fixed horizon. It limits expected positive shortfall, not every episode's loss or future-market risk.

\section{Literature review}
ResSafe freezes a performance policy and learns residual safety corrections in robotics~\cite{ressafe}. RiPO already applies barrier-function risk corrections to RL portfolios~\cite{ripo}. Counterfactual safety compares harm against an alternative policy~\cite{harm}, while portfolio regret learning uses future-looking oracle allocations~\cite{regret}. Thus, modular risk control, counterfactual comparison, and regret are established ideas. The proposed application component is a positive-shortfall budget relative to an executable frozen trading policy, estimated from paired self-financing account trajectories. Its exact originality requires further literature verification.

\section{Main ideas and novelty}
\textbf{Personalized residual adaptation.} Train a nominal PPO policy for net log growth, then freeze its parameters and deployment action rule. Let $z_t^\phi$ denote the controlled account state. Its nominal recommendation is corrected by
\begin{equation}
 w_t=\operatorname{Proj}_{\Delta^4}\left[
 \pi_0(h_t,z_t^\phi)+\delta_\phi(o_t,\pi_0(h_t,z_t^\phi))\right].
\end{equation}
The observation $o_t$ includes market features, both accounts' current states, relative cumulative growth, and time remaining. The untouched reference queries $\pi_0$ on its own state $z_t^0$. Target weights become feasible orders under the same execution rules. Future prices enter training feedback only; a deployed shadow account uses observed prices, not future outcomes.

\textbf{Budgeted economic intervention.} Unlike a signed mean-return floor, $\mathbb E[(G_0-G_\phi)_+]\leq\epsilon$ prevents outperformance in some episodes from cancelling shortfall in others. Unlike an action-norm penalty, it measures realized simulated opportunity cost. This is a proposed Type II application extension, not a new general safety principle. The central test is whether it improves drawdown--shortfall trade-offs relative to the signed-floor formulation and fine-tuning.

\section{Expected contributions}
The project will deliver a paired-account formulation and implementation of budgeted risk intervention; a controlled comparison separating the constraint's effect from the residual architecture; and an IntelliQuant replication assessment. The hypothesis is that positive-shortfall budgeting limits costly over-intervention while allowing drawdown reduction. Results may instead favor fine-tuning or conventional overlays. No policy transfer requirement, guaranteed return preservation, or established state-of-the-art improvement is claimed.

\section{Detailed plan}
\textbf{Week 1: data and simulator.} Verify IntelliQuant history access, supported extraction, and research-use rights~\cite{iq}. Public documentation supports JavaScript backtesting and result downloads; bulk training-data export and Python training remain unverified. Train locally using a supported route or separately licensed data. Fix ETF eligibility using historical information and audit next-open fills, distributions, price adjustments, turnover costs, and cash feasibility. Verify that bypassing the residual reproduces the reference exactly.

\textbf{Week 2: reference and paired feedback.} Train a small nominal PPO policy and freeze it. Implement fixed-horizon paired episodes, maximum drawdown, and terminal positive shortfall. Use identical market paths with separate account transitions. Test that gains never produce negative shortfall and that early episode termination cannot erase losses. Keep current shadow-account information causal.

\textbf{Week 3: constrained adaptation.} Optimize a Lagrangian $\mathbb E[D_T]+\lambda(\mathbb E[L_\phi]-\epsilon)$, increasing $\lambda\geq0$ on budget violations. Use undiscounted finite episodes and terminal shortfall costs. Compare the unchanged policy, residual learning with a signed mean-return floor, the proposed residual controller, fine-tuning with the same positive-shortfall constraint, and simple volatility/drawdown overlays. Match observations, checkpoint initialization, costs, and tuning budgets; include a capacity-matched fine-tuning control. Select a small budget grid using validation data.

\textbf{Weeks 4--5: evaluation.} Use chronological training/validation/test splits and walk-forward evaluation where coverage permits. Purge overlapping episode targets across splits. Report net growth, drawdown, mean positive shortfall, shortfall frequency/severity, budget violations, turnover, and cash exposure. Assess multiple seeds and paired block-bootstrap uncertainty; overlapping windows and seeds are not independent market histories. Calibrate exposure-matched controls on validation data, stress costs, and report training compute. Replicate frozen policies in IntelliQuant if compact inference is feasible and reconcile account discrepancies. VAR scenario training is optional; GRU/RNN models follow only if time permits. Generated paths do not supply independent test evidence.

\textbf{Success criterion.} Establish whether the proposed budget improves the held-out drawdown--shortfall frontier over the signed floor, and whether a residual controller adds value over fine-tuning and simple controls. Document negative results. The required original component is the financial intervention-budget adaptation and its evaluation; verify overlap with prior work before final submission.
